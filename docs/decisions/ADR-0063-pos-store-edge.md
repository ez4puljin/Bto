## Context

The supermarket of Бүтэн-Оргил ХХК has eight tills. Today an Ubuntu PC on the store's LAN (192.168.1.254) runs the old till software's server (tills open it at :7000, its till screen at :7001) and the e-Barimt PosAPI. The owner asked (2026-10-16): «Pos борлуулалт системийг хийе. Pos өөрөө offline горимд ажиллах боломжтой байхаар кассын PC-тэй нэг сүлжээнд байх Ubuntu дээрх тусдаа ажилладаг програм байсан дээр үү?»

Facts that shaped the answer (interview of 2026-10-16, three rounds): the production server will be the cloud droplet of ADR-0026, so an internet outage in Mörön would stop every till that talks to it; the store, the wholesale warehouse and the office share one building and one LAN; every till is a browser (the old software proves it), with a USB keyboard-wedge scanner, an 80 mm Ethernet receipt printer with the cash drawer on it, and a standalone Khan Bank card terminal; without internet the cashiers must still sell for cash and card, open and close shifts and reckon the cash; a stale catalog is acceptable; the e-Barimt receipt is legally required for every sale and the store's PosAPI issues it on that same PC; the owner never wants a till to refuse a sale because the system's shelf balance is lower than the shelf.

The exploration of `main` showed why the centre cannot simply be copied to the store: bigint identity keys everywhere, gapless `number_sequences` per database, reference rows written at every boot, database-backed sessions on every request, one `DATABASE_URL` per process, append-only tables with guard triggers, moving-average costing that depends on posting order, a same-origin web with `connect-src 'self'`. ADR-0020 had ruled offline out and ADR-0022 had planned a Windows print agent because the cloud api could not reach the printers; both premises fall with a server on the store's LAN.

## Decision

1. **A store edge server.** The tills talk only to a process on the store's LAN: the same `bto-api` image run as a third role, `ROLE=edge`, with its own module tree (`apps/api/src/edge/**`), its own Drizzle schema and migrations and its own PostgreSQL on the Ubuntu PC. Online and offline are one path: the edge writes a document locally first and replays it to the centre afterwards. The edge is built in the next session (S2); this session builds what it replays into.
2. **The centre is the system of record.** A `pos` module (`apps/api/src/modules/pos`, `MODULE_DEPS.pos = ['identity', 'catalog', 'pricing', 'inventory', 'finance']`) owns the store's settings, edge nodes, tills, cashiers, shifts, sales with their lines and tenders, shortfalls and refused documents. Stock leaves the ledger here (`POS_SALE` from `SALESFLOOR`, `RETURN_IN` back onto it, or onto `DAMAGED`), money is booked here (ADR-0064), reports and the assistant's views read here. The sales module gives up the POS tables architecture §4 had sketched for it.
3. **Ids and numbers.** Every POS document carries a `client_id` uuid the till gave it; the centre keeps `UNIQUE (edge_node_id, client_id)` and answers a repeated document with the row it already has (DUPLICATE). The visible number is allocated on the edge per till and Ulaanbaatar day — `К3-261016-0045` for a sale, `К3-261016-Э1` for a shift — and stored verbatim, unique per edge. The centre's bigint ids stay central.
4. **Replay in order.** The edge posts batches of up to 50 documents to `POST /api/v1/pos/sync/events` in the order they happened (a shift is opened before its sales, closed after them) and reports itself on `POST /api/v1/pos/sync/heartbeat`. Each document is applied in its own transaction and answered APPLIED, DUPLICATE or REJECTED. A refusal the edge could not cure by waiting (a product or cashier the centre does not know, arithmetic that does not add up, a return outside its sale) is logged in `pos_sync_rejections` with the document, and the stream continues; the admin replays it after fixing the data. A failure that waiting cures (a shelf under count, a lock) is returned as the error and the edge resends the batch. The till's prices are authoritative: the centre recomputes every line's subtotal, total and VAT from the till's unit price and quantity, checks the VAT rate against the company's rates on the sale's day and the tenders against the total, and refuses on a difference (`POS_TOTALS_MISMATCH`, `POS_TAX_MISMATCH`); it never re-prices.
5. **A sale is never refused for stock.** Before a sale posts, the centre locks the SALESFLOOR balances of its products; what the sale needs beyond them is adjusted in first by an automatic `POS_SHORTFALL` adjustment (one line per product, posted through inventory's `postSystemInTx`, a reason users cannot pick), so the sale posts in full, the bin never goes negative and the storekeeper gets a `pos_shortfalls` row per product to check (counted, moved, explained). The ledger's effective date is the centre's posting time; the sale keeps its own `sold_at` and `sold_day`.
6. **Who is who.** Cashiers are staff users with a `pos_cashiers` row: a six-digit PIN hashed with argon2id keyed by `POS_PIN_PEPPER` (falling back to `AUTH_PEPPER` until the owner sets it), set by an admin and never shown again; the hash travels to the edge in its snapshot (S2), the key does not. New permissions `pos.read` (office, accountant), `pos.manage` (admin), `pos.till.operate` (cashiers) and `pos.till.supervise`; a new system role `POS_SUPERVISOR` («Ахлах кассчин») approves returns and no-sale drawer opens by PIN at the till (owner 2026-10-16). The edge authenticates to the centre with a token `tgs_<node id>_<secret>` issued once in the admin and kept as a sha-256 in `pos_edge_nodes`; its routes are `@Public()` for the staff guard and carry `EdgeTokenGuard` instead. Documents are audited with the actor `edge:<code>` and the cashier on the row.
7. **Returns.** A return names the sale it takes goods back from and, per line, the sold line; it is accepted within `return_window_days` of the sale (14 by default), at the sold unit price, for no more than what is left of the sold line (`returned_base` grows), with a supervisor's approval when `supervisor_required` is set. Goods go back onto SALESFLOOR, or onto DAMAGED when the cashier marked the item damaged.
8. **The admin's «Супермаркет».** Pages for the settings (the STORE warehouse, the cash box the shifts' cash is booked into, the return window, the supervisor rule), the edge nodes and their tokens, the tills (code, printer host and port, drawer, pairing code for S2), the cashiers and their PINs, the shifts with their Z facts and the hand-over, the sales and returns with lines and tenders, the shortfalls and the refused documents. A development-only «Туршилтын касс» prices a cart at RETAIL on the centre and runs it through the same accept path, so the owner walks the whole flow before the edge exists.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Tills call the cloud api directly, an LTE router as backup (ADR-0020) | An outage of either link stops eight tills; the owner wants the store to sell without the internet. |
| The central api image on the store PC against a copy of the central database | Id and number collisions, boot-time reference writes, database sessions, append-only guards and costing order all collide (see Context); two databases claiming to be the truth. |
| A separate application `apps/pos-edge` | Duplicates `common/**` (config, database, errors, logging, idempotency) for a solo maintainer; a third role of one image shares them. |
| The POS inside the sales module (architecture §4 as drafted) | Different documents, actors, numbering and money; sales would import finance and inventory for a flow it does not own. |
| Refusing a sale the ledger cannot cover, or letting the bin go negative | The first stops a customer at the till over a bookkeeping gap; the second breaks every invariant the ledger has. An explicit, reviewable adjustment keeps both. |
| Service accounts in identity with a new `AuthContext.kind` | Touches the global guard, the idempotency interceptor and every consumer of `AuthContext`; a route-local guard for one caller is enough until a second kind of machine caller appears. |

## Consequences

**Positive.** The tills never depend on the internet; one code path for online and offline; the centre keeps its invariants (ids, numbers, ledger, money) and every till document is traceable to its uuid, number, cashier and shift; the owner can test the whole flow from the admin before the store hardware is touched.

**Negative.** A second production machine to install, back up and upgrade (the runbook comes with S5); a sale replayed after an outage posts with the centre's date, not the till's; the shelf's recorded balance can run ahead of reality until the storekeeper acts on the shortfalls; the edge's snapshot can lag a price change by its pull interval.

**Follow-ups.** S2: the edge role, the snapshot pull, the kiosk's first screens, the round-trip test. S3: printing and the e-Barimt provider on the edge (ADR-0065). S4: returns, parked baskets, price check, B2B at the till, the shortfall and rejection pages, the assistant's views. S5: the store deployment and the pilot till. The owner decides before S5 where the centre runs on the cut-over day (the droplet early, or the store PC carrying both roles until go-live).
